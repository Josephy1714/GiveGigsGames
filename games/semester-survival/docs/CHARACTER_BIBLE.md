# Character Bible

## Visual identity rules
- Establish a model sheet before production sprites: front, three-quarter, side, back, expressions and scale reference.
- Keep head/body ratio, eye placement, jaw shape, hair silhouette, skin palette, outfit seams and accessories consistent.
- Use a limited authored palette and controlled line weight. Avoid glossy plastic skin, random gradients, over-rendered pores and inconsistent facial features.
- Hands are posed intentionally. Simplify a silhouette rather than inventing unreadable fingers.
- Each character has a recognizable silhouette at thumbnail size.

## Initial cast roles
- Player: customizable presentation within a fixed animation-compatible base rig.
- Roommate: practical, friendly and occasionally blunt; provides residence context.
- Coursemate: study partner with their own schedule and priorities.
- Food vendor: township service character; prices and stock stay consistent.
- Class representative: communicates deadlines, notices and group-work opportunities.
- Lecturer: appears in academic spaces; dialogue is concise and role-appropriate.

Names, biographies and final model sheets must be authored before final dialogue implementation. Never randomly rename a character between scenes.

## Dialogue
Choices should express distinct human intentions, not paraphrases. Avoid stereotypes. Relationship changes come from authored actions and are stored explicitly.
