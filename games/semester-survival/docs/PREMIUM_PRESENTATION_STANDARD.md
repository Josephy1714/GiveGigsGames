# Premium Presentation Standard

This is the single visual and sensory quality contract for every section of Semester Survival. It applies to residence, township, campus, city, characters, animation, interfaces, cinematics, effects, audio and transitions. The user's two campus references establish the environmental-art finish: cinematic composition, confident architecture, polished anime-background rendering, detailed natural surroundings, coherent light and depth. Each section translates that quality into its own purpose and a credible Kenyan setting.

## 1. Global art direction

- Preserve the established 2.5D exploration format. Compose scenes for side-on play with independent depth movement, readable walkable planes, intentional parallax and a clear focal point.
- Use high-resolution human-authored/commissioned source artwork with clean perspective, controlled detail, coherent material response, consistent colour grading and intentional lighting.
- Ground all places in a believable Kenyan university and Nairobi student experience. Local identity comes from architecture, street furniture, signage, vegetation, transport, shops, clothing and lived-in details—not pasted stereotypes or copied foreign scenery.
- Every district must have a distinct identity while sharing one coherent art direction. Do not make all districts look like the same generic campus.
- Maintain visual hierarchy: gameplay routes, doors, interactables, NPC silhouettes and signage remain readable at gameplay scale.
- No generated placeholder art, fake character sprites, unapproved assets or unrelated fallback imagery.

## 2. District-by-district standard

- **Residence:** intimate, lived-in and personal. Distinct room ownership, practical furniture, believable wear, warm interior light, corridors and courtyard circulation. Avoid a luxury-hotel look.
- **Township:** dense but legible Nairobi student neighbourhood. Shops, food kiosk, barber, cyber/print hub, rentals, footpaths and transport stage need individually designed storefronts, signage and believable street activity. Keep the playable route readable.
- **Campus:** aspirational institutional spaces with strong architectural identity, landscaped courtyards, academic buildings, library, labs, lecture spaces and student commons. Balance monumental establishing views with human-scale details.
- **City:** broader urban scale, layered roads and buildings, transport nodes, commercial frontage and distinct destinations. Use depth and atmosphere to communicate scale without overwhelming navigation.

Every district needs a wide establishing composition, close interaction areas, background/midground/foreground layers, authored hotspots, collision review, lighting continuity and weather compatibility.

## 3. Character production

- Each recurring character receives a consistent turnaround, expression sheet, wardrobe specification, palette, height comparison and approved animation atlas.
- Faces, anatomy, proportions, costume construction, hair, accessories and skin palette must remain stable across every frame and scene.
- Jay, Amina, Einstein, Ernest and Sumeya are the main cast; existing supporting cast remain. Amina and Sumeya retain distinct modest contemporary Muslim clothing and individually designed hijab styling.
- Character art is separate from environment plates. Do not bake anonymous crowds into scenery as a substitute for NPCs.
- No character is called complete until artwork, animation coverage, credits, approval and in-game scale review pass.

## 4. Animation and camera

- Movement must show anticipation, weight transfer, contact, momentum, follow-through and recovery. Walk, jog, sprint, turns, jumps, slides, dodges, stairs, interactions and idle acting need distinct authored motion.
- Feet must plant without skating; body weight must respond to acceleration and landing; hair, clothing and backpack secondary motion must follow primary movement.
- Camera follow, transitions, dialogue framing and environmental movement must feel deliberate and stable. Avoid abrupt cuts, jitter, clipping and distracting camera shake.
- Runtime code controls and blends authored animation; it does not invent missing character artwork.

## 5. Interface and interaction

- HUD, phone, menus, dialogue, map, academic portal and results screens share a coherent premium design system, typography, spacing, icon language, motion and sound feedback.
- Keep the world visible. Use contextual HUD elements and clear hierarchy instead of covering exploration with permanent panels.
- Every control has a real, testable consequence; all screens support keyboard, touch, readable contrast and reduced motion.
- Feedback must explain what happened and why. Never show a success state for an action that did not complete.

## 6. Effects, weather and sound

- Weather changes atmosphere, light and authored environmental response while preserving scene identity and navigation readability.
- Effects such as dust, rain, splashes, contact sparks and environmental particles must be restrained, spatially grounded and tied to actual events. No decorative effect should obscure gameplay.
- Sound design should distinguish residence, campus, township and city through appropriate ambience, footsteps, surfaces, doors, transport and interaction cues. Music and effects must be mixed so dialogue and important feedback remain clear.
- Do not claim an effect, animation or sound is implemented until its asset and runtime path exist and have been tested.

## 7. Performance and delivery

- Author crisp high-resolution source artwork; adapt runtime resolution, texture size, effects and frame pacing to the device. “4K quality” is a source/detail target, not a promise that every phone renders at native 4K.
- Preserve responsive controls and stable frame pacing on supported desktop and mobile devices. Use measured budgets for textures, draw calls, animation atlases and effects.
- All assets must be local, credited, licensed, dimension-checked and within the repository package budget. Offline play remains mandatory.
- Every asset and feature passes visual review at native gameplay scale, device QA, accessibility checks and regression tests.

## 8. Readiness language

A design brief or blueprint is not finished artwork. A runtime module is not a finished player experience. Use explicit states: planned, authored, integrated, visually reviewed, device-tested and approved. Never promote a feature to a later state without evidence.
