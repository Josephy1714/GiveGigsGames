# Game Design Document

## Product identity
Working title: Semester Survival. Genre: narrative university-life adventure / life simulation. Single-player browser game for desktop and mobile. Eight-semester campaign with replayable choices and multiple graduation outcomes.

The player experiences university as a connected life, not a score-attack obstacle course. The fantasy is building a life: attending classes, making friends, managing limited money, learning skills, navigating setbacks and deciding what kind of graduate to become.

## Design pillars
- A living student world: spaces have people, routines, services, schedules and reasons to return.
- Agency with consequences: choices affect grades, finances, energy, stress, relationships and opportunities.
- Expressive anime presentation: readable silhouettes, controlled proportions, clear facial acting and consistent identity.
- Movement with feel: acceleration, deceleration, planted turns, jump arcs, landing recovery and secondary motion.
- Kenyan university texture: campus routines, transport stages, kiosks, printing shops, cyber cafés, affordable food, rent and student budgeting, represented respectfully.
- Accessible depth: meaningful systems introduced in understandable layers.

## Narrative
The player arrives as a first-year student with a modest budget, an unfamiliar campus and an open future. The story follows eight semesters. There is no single prescribed personality or success route. Players may prioritize academic excellence, friendships, creative work, entrepreneurship, employment, wellbeing or balance.

## Core loop
1. Check the phone: timetable, messages, money, tasks and map.
2. Choose a plan for the day.
3. Travel through residence, township and campus.
4. Attend classes, study, work, socialize or handle errands.
5. Make decisions during conversations and activities.
6. Review consequences and prepare for the next day.
7. Complete semester assessments and advance.

## World
- Residence: room, sleep, study, storage, roommates and recovery.
- Township: food kiosks, shops, barber, cyber/printing, rentals, transport stage, side hustles and social encounters.
- University: lecture halls, library, labs, administration, lecturer offices, student commons, clubs and events.
- City: internships, errands, shopping, entertainment, interviews and special missions.

Keep the world compact and dense. Avoid a huge empty open world. District transitions should be legible; travel time should create decisions rather than tedious downtime.

## Eight-semester arc
| Semester | Dramatic focus | New systems |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Arrival, orientation and belonging | Navigation, timetable, basic budget |
| 2 | Finding rhythm | Study planning, friendships, clubs |
| 3 | Pressure and independence | Group work, deadlines, side income |
| 4 | Mid-degree reassessment | CATs, specialization, stress management |
| 5 | Higher-level work | Labs, projects, mentorship |
| 6 | Opportunity and trade-offs | Internship applications, portfolio |
| 7 | Final-year momentum | Research, capstone, career preparation |
| 8 | Completion and transition | Final assessments, interviews, graduation |

Each semester needs authored story beats and objectives. This framework is not a substitute for mission content.

## Outcomes
Endings combine academic standing, wellbeing, finances, relationships, completed projects and opportunities. Possible outcomes include distinction, balanced graduate, founder/creator, employed graduate, delayed completion with recovery, and personal-growth ending. Avoid a binary winner/loser ending.

## Skill demonstrations
Tutorials show a poor choice and a better choice, label the outcome and let the player try. Red crosses and green ticks require a real explained consequence. Tutorials are skippable and replayable.

## Vertical-slice gate
Prove one connected route between residence, township and campus; one playable character with readable locomotion; at least three meaningful NPC interactions; one class/study activity; a working phone; persistent save; and a clear day/semester loop.
